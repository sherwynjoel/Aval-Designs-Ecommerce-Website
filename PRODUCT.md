# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

Primary: women shopping for bridal and occasion wear (weddings, receptions, engagements, festive events), roughly 22–45, browsing mostly on mobile, often weeks or months ahead of the event. They are comparing boutiques on trust and craftsmanship before committing to a custom, higher-value garment (₹10,000+), so photography quality, clarity of the customization/measurement process, and visible proof of quality matter more than price-first browsing. Secondary: family members (mothers, sisters) researching or ordering on behalf of the bride/occasion wearer, sometimes managing multiple people's measurements for one event.

## Product Purpose

Aval Designs is a premium women's boutique for bridal and occasion wear, combining ready-to-shop pieces with full custom tailoring (measurements, fabric, embroidery, design choices). The site exists to let a customer discover a piece, trust the boutique enough to commit to a custom order sight-unseen on fit, and complete that order (or a consultation enquiry) with minimal friction. Success is a completed custom order or consultation request from a customer who felt confident about fit and craftsmanship before paying.

## Positioning

Bridal and occasion wear made to your exact measurements, without sacrificing runway-level craftsmanship or editorial presentation.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary CTA: "Shop Collection" (browse ready pieces and occasion/bridal lines); secondary CTA: "Explore New Arrivals" / "Create Your Design" (custom-order path) for visitors not ready to buy off-the-shelf.
- The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds: "This boutique will make something made-for-me, and I can trust the process."
- Belief ladder: (1) the craftsmanship and photography are genuinely premium, not generic e-commerce → (2) I can get this made to my exact measurements without guesswork → (3) the customization/ordering process is clear and trustworthy → (4) it's worth committing budget to for my occasion.
- Proof on hand: none supplied yet. Prototype will use realistic-looking placeholder testimonials/reviews clearly meant to be replaced with real customer proof before launch.

## Brand Personality

Minimal luxury, elegant, feminine, modern, editorial-fashion — quiet confidence rather than loud sales energy. Voice is warm and personal (this is about a customer's own occasion and body, not a generic SKU) but never gushing or exclamation-heavy. Photography and whitespace carry the brand more than copy or UI chrome does.

## Anti-references

Not a generic e-commerce template: avoid overly colorful UI, excessive gradients, cheap/heavy card shadows, excessive rounded corners, cluttered layouts, over-animation, generic Bootstrap-looking components, excessive iconography, and small/cropped product imagery. Not a mass-market fast-fashion feel — this is closer to a designer boutique than a marketplace.

## Design Principles

- Photography and whitespace lead; UI chrome recedes. Every section should feel like it could be a page from a fashion editorial, not a SaaS landing page.
- Never hide the path to "made for you." Customization and measurements are a core value prop, not a buried feature — surface them without turning the homepage into a form.
- Trust is built visually before it's built with copy. Craftsmanship, fabric, and fit get shown, not just claimed.
- Keep motion and UI restrained so the imagery and typography stay the stars — subtle reveals and micro-interactions only.
- Design for the bridal/occasion decision journey: a considered, high-stakes purchase, not an impulse buy.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Standard WCAG AA target: body text ≥4.5:1 contrast, large text ≥3:1, full keyboard navigation, respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. No additional accessibility requirements specified yet.
